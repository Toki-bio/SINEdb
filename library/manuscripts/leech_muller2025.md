## Summary
This paper presents the first molecular description and characterization of transposable elements (TEs) in leech genomes, focusing on SINEs, LINEs, and LTR retrotransposons (all class I retrotransposons) across several leech taxa (phylum Annelida, class Clitellata). The entry point is the discovery of short interspersed nuclear elements (SINEs) that have transposed into hirudin and hirudin-like factor (HLF) genes of the medicinal leech Hirudo verbana, where a SINE insertion had altered the structure of the HV1 gene. The author characterizes a set of four H. verbana SINEs (HvSINE1-4), then extends the analysis to homologous HvSINE1-like elements in Hirudinaria manillensis, Whitmania pigra, Haemopis sanguisuga, and Hirudo nipponia, and additionally identifies LINEs (HvLINE1-4, HmLINE1, WpLINE1) and LTR retrotransposons (WpLTR1, HvLTR1). What is genuinely new is that some of the newly described elements display unique structural features compared to the archetypes of their respective groups, making leeches (non-model organisms) a productive source of new TE diversity.

## Results & Discussion
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION (detailed synthesis). 3.1 HvSINE1: The study grew out of earlier work on hirudin and hirudin-like factor (HLF) gene structures (HV1/HV2/HV3 of H. medicinalis and H. verbana), all sharing a highly conserved exon/intron architecture. When the H. verbana whole-genome assembly (BioProject PRJNA55103, GCA_020137395.1, the Paulsen et al. biosample) became available, the HV1 gene of that particular biosample showed a remarkable structural difference versus both the author's own sequenced HV1 genes (H. verbana KX215734.1/KX215735.1; H. medicinalis KR066930.1/KR066931.1) and the assemblies of Kvist et al. The difference was an inserted mobile element within the gene, identified as a SINE and named HvSINE1. HvSINE1 (~290 bp) displays the canonical t-SINE architecture: a tRNA-derived 'head' carrying the pol III promoter (A and B boxes), a central body/core domain, and a 3' LINE-related segment; it is flanked by target site duplications (TSDs). The author discusses the T+/T- classification (presence/absence of the polyadenylation signal AATAAA and the pol III terminator TCTTT) and notes the structural context for SINE/LINE pairing.

3.2-3.4 HvSINE2-4: Building on HvSINE1, three further H. verbana SINEs (HvSINE2, HvSINE3, HvSINE4) were identified and characterized. They share the same general plan (tRNA-derived pol III head, central body, LINE-related 3' tail, flanking TSDs) but differ in size and in the degree to which the 3' tail resembles particular LINE partners; the author notes that several of the newly described elements display structural features that depart from the archetype of their group (the 'unique structural features' highlighted in the abstract), reinforcing the value of non-model leech genomes.

Extension to other leech taxa: Homologous HvSINE1-like elements were sought across available leech genomes/transcriptomes. The analysis extended to Hirudinaria manillensis (GCA_034509925.1; six elements Hman_SINE1-6, plus the previously named HmSINE_V2), Whitmania pigra (GCA_041430665.1; Wpig_SINE1-4), Haemopis sanguisuga (Hsan_SINE1-4) and Hirudo nipponia (Hnip_SINE1-3). For each species a SINE consensus was derived and aligned (Supplementary Figures S3-S8), and per-species consensus sequences are given as plain text before the alignment blocks. The conservation of the A/B-box promoter and the tRNA-derived head across taxa, together with cross-species sequence similarity, indicates that these are homologous t-SINE lineages shared among leeches rather than independent acquisitions; UPGMA/Jukes-Cantor phylogenetic trees (bootstrap 10,000) were used to relate the elements and to relate the SINE 3' tails to candidate LINE partners. The implication for hirudin biology is discussed: insertion of a SINE (as exemplified by HvSINE1 in the H. verbana HV1 gene) can alter hirudin gene structure and may affect expression of the thrombin inhibitor, the one leech-derived compound in clinical use.

LINEs: Class I autonomous partners were also characterized. Four H. verbana LINEs (HvLINE1-4) plus HmLINE1 (H. manillensis) and WpLINE1 (W. pigra) were identified. These ~3-6 kb elements contain ORFs encoding the expected retrotransposition machinery - reverse transcriptase (RT) and an endonuclease (APE/apurinic and/or RLE/restriction-like), with accessory domains such as RNase H (RH), the CCHC zinc-finger knuckle, and an RNA-recognition motif (RRM, built from RNP1/RNP2 sub-motifs). The presence and arrangement of these domains were used to assign the LINEs to superfamilies per Wicker et al.; the shared 3' sequences between SINEs and their cognate LINEs (stringent vs relaxed SINE/LINE pairing, the latter via polyA) are discussed as the basis for the SINE/LINE partnerships inferred in the leech taxa.

LTR retrotransposons: Two LTR elements were characterized - WpLTR1 (W. pigra) and HvLTR1 (H. verbana). They show the hallmark LTR-retrotransposon organization: flanking long terminal repeats enclosing gag and pol (RT, RNase H, integrase (IN), aspartic protease (AP)). Together with the SINEs and LINEs, these confirm that representatives of all three targeted TE classes (SINE, LINE, LTR retrotransposon) are present in leech genomes. The author concludes (Discussion/Conclusions) that leeches, as non-model organisms with newly accessible genomes, are a rich and still largely untapped source of TE diversity, that several described elements possess non-canonical structural features relative to archetypes, and that further genome-scale annotation (e.g., RepeatModeler/RepeatMasker) is needed to quantify copy numbers and reconstruct the full TE complement across leeches.
