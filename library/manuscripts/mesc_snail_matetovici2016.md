## Summary
This paper reports the discovery and characterization of SINEs (Short INterspersed Elements) in gastropod and bivalve mollusks, beginning with five SINE families (Lsa_1\u2013Lsa_5) found in Littorina saxatilis (rough periwinkle) BAC sequences and then extended by homology searches across 16+ gastropod and bivalve species. The genuinely novel contributions are two new SINE superfamilies defined by central conserved domains: the MESC superfamily (MESC = Romanian MElc/snail + SCoica/mussel), whose ~95 bp central domain is shared across Caenogastropoda, Vetigastropoda and all four bivalve subclasses but appears absent from Heterobranchia/Patellogastropoda; and the Snail superfamily, restricted to Caenogastropoda, whose central domain is related to the Nin-DC domain. The authors additionally extend the known ranges of V-SINE-related elements (proposing a conserved "Vhc" sub-domain shared by mollusks and arthropods) and of CORE SINEs (now found in gastropods and bivalves), and they describe "LP-SINEs" \u2014 short retrotransposons whose head/promoter is derived from a LINE 5'UTR rather than from a small cellular RNA, a notable departure from the usual tRNA/5S-derived SINE head.

## Results & Discussion
DETAILED SYNTHESIS OF RESULTS AND DISCUSSION. The work begins from BAC-derived data of the caenogastropod Littorina saxatilis (family Littorinidae). Five SINE families (Lsa_1 through Lsa_5) were identified via PILER. Lsa_2 and Lsa_3 had been reported earlier by others; Lsa_1, Lsa_4 and Lsa_5 are new.

Lsa_1: Sixteen copies were found on the L. saxatilis BAC sequences; six of these are 5'-truncated. Target site duplications (TSDs) could be identified for ten elements (both full-length and truncated). The 5' portion of Lsa_1 folds into a tRNA-like secondary structure with discernible A and B boxes (RNA polymerase III promoter boxes). Because Crassostrea gigas is the only mollusk with annotated tRNA genes, the Lsa_1 5' domain was compared to the C. gigas Ensembl set, identifying tRNA-Arg as the most likely head source; a homologous tRNA-Arg could also be retrieved from a L. saxatilis short read archive. Among tRNA-derived SINE heads, the Lsa_1 5' domain is most closely related to the sea urchin SINEs SINE2-1_SP and SINE2-2_SP (Kapitonov & Jurka 2005). The tRNA-unrelated region shows no homology to known SINE or LINE sequences. Lsa_1 ends in CAAA tandem repeats.

MESC superfamily: A BLAST search with the Lsa_1 consensus retrieved homologous sequences from 16 gastropod and bivalve species, with homology covering the tRNA-related head and the 5' part of the tRNA-unrelated region. Sufficient flanking sequence to identify TSDs was available for 10 of the 16 species; for the rest (and for Argopecten EST/nr sequences) contigs were assembled from partial short-read and GenBank data. Alignment of consensus/contig sequences revealed a ~95 bp segment immediately downstream of the head region (marked by an arrowhead in Fig. 1) that is highly conserved in Caenogastropoda and Vetigastropoda and in SINEs from all four bivalve subclasses: Paleoheterodonta, Heterodonta, Pteriomorpha and Protobranchia (Fig. 2). This central block appears restricted to SINEs in those taxa; it could not be identified in the available sequences of Heterobranchia or Patellogastropoda (the other two major gastropod clades), nor in other mollusk classes. Because all species carrying the domain are snails or mussels, the authors named it the MESC domain (Romanian MElc = snail, SCoica = mussel). At its 3' end, the MESC domain is followed by an approximately 50 bp region shared between bivalve SINEs and the vetigastropod abalone Haliotis (boxed in Fig. 1; dark grey boxes in Fig. 2). SINE tails are shared among MESC elements in Paleoheterodonta, Heterodonta and Protobranchia, implying mobilization by the same as-yet-unidentified LINE. Terminal tandem repeats are most frequently (CA)n or (CAAA)n; Haliotis MESC SINEs end in (CACCT)n and Bathymodiolus in (CACT)n, with expansion of a (CA)n microsatellite observed within Haliotis MESC SINEs.

Pectinoid (scallop) MESC SINEs: Potential MESC-containing SINEs were recovered from pectinoid bivalves, with homology extending up- and downstream of the MESC domain. For Farrer's scallop (Chlamys farreri/Azumapecten farreri), 16 full-length-spanning sequences were retrieved and TSDs determined for eleven. The scallop elements are ~520 bp; the 5'-most 68 bp is repeat-masked by Censor and is similar to the first 68 bp (5'UTR) of Nimb-17_LMi, an autonomous non-LTR retrotransposon of Locusta migratoria belonging to the Nimb clade of I-like non-LTR retrotransposons (found in fish, mollusks, sea squirts, sea urchins and insects). This LINE-derived 5' domain is followed by an 88 bp CT-rich region of unknown origin. The acquisition of upstream sequence is compared to Deu-SINEs, in which 5S rRNA fused to the 5' end of a tRNA-derived head took over promoter function (Nishihara et al. 2006). The LINE-derived sequence can either be added to, or replace, the tRNA-derived head (Fig. 3B). Partial/contig sequences also indicated MESC-domain SINEs with a LINE-derived 5' domain in Argopecten irradians, Mizuhopecten yessoensis, Placopecten magellanicus and Pecten maximus transcriptome shotgun assemblies (with possible misassembly across similar internal sequences).

LP-SINEs (LINE promoter SINEs): Because all characterized SINE heads derive from small cellular RNAs and are transcribed by Pol III, SINEs whose head/promoter comes from a LINE are proposed to be possibly Pol II-transcribed. Variable-size TSDs and 3' terminal short tandem repeats (SINE-typical) nonetheless argue for LINE-dependent mobilization. LINE-derived 5' domains occur in the L. saxatilis Lsa_2 family (nine copies on BACs; six with variable-size TSDs) and in Haliotis (H. diversicolor), Saccostrea and the pectinoid MESC SINEs; Haliotis, Saccostrea and Chlamys LP-SINEs share a tail sequence, suggesting a shared (unidentified) mobilizing LINE. The authors coin "LP-SINEs" (LINE promoter SINEs). Experimental work is needed to confirm the promoter function and the transcribing polymerase. A canonical polyA signal precedes the tandem repeats in Lsa_2 and Chlamys MESC SINEs, resembling Pol II-transcribed LINEs such as UnaL2 and ZfL3; Haliotis/Ostrea carry polyA-signal variants (AATATA, ACTAAA) shown functional in humans (Beaudoing et al. 2000), while the Saccostrea Sgl_LP hexamer (AGTATA) has not been reported as a polyA signal. Outside mollusks, a similar LINE-headed SINE family was identified in the Mediterranean fruit fly Ceratitis capitata (data not shown). LP-SINEs are presented as the only known instance of LINE sequence forming the SINE head (heads usually being tRNA or 5S rRNA). Template switching of LINE reverse transcriptase is invoked as the mechanism for recruitment/exchange of modules. Homology among LP-SINEs (L. saxatilis, Haliotis, Saccostrea, pectinoids) is restricted to the first ~70 bp (Fig. 3C); the same ~70 bp domain is also the only region of homology among three LINE families carrying it at their 5' ends \u2014 two Nimb-clade LINEs (Nimb-17_LMi, I-3_DR) and the painted-turtle RTE element RTE-9_CPB. In the two Nimb LINEs the 5'UTR extends a further ~300 bp with no homology; in the turtle RTE the conserved block is roughly half the 5'UTR. This is paralleled by trypanosomatid ingi/L1Tc elements, whose shared 77 bp fragment has both promoter and ribozyme activity, and by crocodilian Tx1 LINEs sharing U1/U2-snRNA-derived 5' ends with SINEU families (Kojima 2015).

V-domain / Vhc SINEs: Searching with the Lsa_1 head retrieved SINEs from only two bivalves \u2014 Tegillarca granosa (Arcoida) and Meretrix meretrix (Veneroida) \u2014 each combined with different bodies/tails. The M. meretrix tail is shared with another bivalve SINE superfamily that unexpectedly shows homology to vertebrate V-SINEs (Ogiwara et al. 2002). Using the V-domain as query, a second bivalve V-SINE-homologous superfamily was identified; in both, homology to the vertebrate V-domain covers its 5' end and terminates at the position of the 3' end of the lamprey Lam1 V-domain. A highly conserved 39 bp block at the 3' end of the V-homology is, on average, ~90% identical (first superfamily) and ~78% identical (second superfamily) to the consensus across all fish V-SINEs of Ogiwara et al. The same sub-domain is >80% identical to the "all fish" consensus in SINEs of a gastropod (Strombus gigas), a protobranch bivalve (Yoldia limatula) and arthropods (Empusa pennata, Thermobia domestica); the authors therefore propose the name "Vhc" (V highly conserved) SINEs. The first bivalve Vhc superfamily (Vhc_1) occurs in Pteriomorpha \u2014 Ostreidae (C. gigas), Arcidae (T. granosa) and Pectinidae (Chlamys farreri, M. yessoensis, Nodopecten subnodosus, A. irradians); its head is tRNA-derived (best C. gigas match tRNA-Glu), and it lacks well-defined 3' tandem repeats except in C. gigas, where some end in (CAGTT)n (TSDs could be identified).
