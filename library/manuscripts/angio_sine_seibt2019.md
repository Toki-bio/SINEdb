## Summary
This paper defines a new SINE superfamily — the Angio-SINEs — united by a previously unrecognized, highly conserved 3'-terminal motif that the authors name the "Angio-domain," found across angiosperm (higher plant) genomes. By comparing 60 SINEBase plant SINE sequences plus 28 and 32 SINE families from Amaranthaceae and Poaceae, the authors identify this shared distal-3' domain, then use BLASTN searches of the NCBI WGS database (across 27 plant orders plus gymnosperms) to map its distribution and reconstruct 24 named Angio-SINE families spanning 13 plant orders. What is genuinely new is (i) the discovery and naming of the Angio-domain as a unifying structural feature at the 3' end (distinct from the central V-, CORE- and Ceph-SINE domains of animal/mobile SINEs), (ii) the assembly of 24 families into one superfamily with consensus sequences, copy-number/full-length estimates and a gene-association analysis, and (iii) the inference of three diversification routes (divergence, dimerization and modular reshuffling) plus a "relaxed" (non-stringent) partner-LINE hypothesis for the superfamily's evolutionary success.

## Results & Discussion
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION (detailed synthesis). The authors set out to find evolutionary links among plant SINEs by comparing 60 SINEBase plant SINE sequences plus 28 and 32 SINE families/subfamilies from Amaranthaceae and Poaceae (Schwichtenberg et al. 2016; Kogler et al. 2017). Using polydot comparisons (EMBOSS, word size 10) they found a shared sequence region at the 3' end that is conserved between several plant SINE families. This region, which they call the "Angio-domain," sits at the distal 3' end of the SINE and its conservation extends right up to the poly(A) tail (the region between the Angio-domain and the poly(A) tail being slightly more diverged). This is structurally distinct from the previously described conserved SINE domains — the V-, CORE- and Ceph-SINE domains of animal SINEs — which lie centrally, downstream of the RNA polymerase III promoter. By contrast the Angio-domain is 3'-terminal, the very end of the element.

Structure and conservation of the Angio-domain. The Angio-domain defines a larger "extended Angio-domain" (eAngio) that also encompasses the Pol III promoter (the tRNA-derived A and B boxes) and a reverse-complementary (RC) region. Strikingly, the A and B box promoter motifs are LESS conserved than the Angio-domain itself, an inversion of the usual expectation that the promoter is the most conserved part of a SINE. RNA structure prediction (Vienna RNAfold, Turner 2004 model at 20°C) of the eAngio region revealed a stem-loop whose stem (the RC region) is structurally constrained, suggesting the conserved secondary structure — not just primary sequence — is under selection. The authors propose that GC-richness stabilizes this RNA secondary structure and the DNA-RNA hybrid that would form during target-primed reverse transcription (TPRT). In all-against-all FLEXIDOT dotplots (word size 12, two mismatches) the Angio-domain appears as a conserved block shared across otherwise divergent families, with MEGA7 p-distance similarity values shown in the top-right triangle of the dotplot matrices.

The 24 families and 13 orders. BLASTN searches (word size 7, no dust/soft-masking) of the NCBI WGS database across 27 plant orders plus gymnosperms recovered Angio-domain hits in 13 plant orders; no Angio-domain was found in gymnosperms, consistent with an angiosperm-specific (hence "Angio-") superfamily. For each order with hits the authors built representative majority-consensus sequences (iterating BLASTN against genome assemblies and alignments including ~200 bp flanks), yielding 24 named Angio-SINE families/subfamilies. Names follow prior conventions (Wenke et al. 2011; Schwichtenberg et al. 2016). The text discusses families including, among others, SolS-Ia, SolS-Ib, SolS-II, SolS-V, SolS-VII, SolS-VIII and SolS-IX (Solanaceae/Solanales, including the previously described TS-SINE of tobacco and SolS-II of potato), EriS-I and EriS-III (Ericales), FabaS-IV (a rosid family showing ~83% similarity to EriS-III but lacking an intact Angio-domain), AmaS-XXII (Amaranthaceae/Caryophyllales), SaliS-IV and SaliS-V, ProtS-II, and RanuS-I (Ranunculales, added after genome assemblies became available Nov 2017). For Piperales, where no genome assembly existed, Angio-domain hits were detected via the NCBI EST database and confirmed by BLASTN against the onekp transcriptome database (v5, accessed 6 March 2019) using SolS-II and RanuS-I as queries.

Copy numbers and full-length members. For each family the authors retrieved full-length copies (requiring ≥95% query-length coverage, with duplicate cross-family hits disambiguated by aligning the hit plus 75 bp flank to all consensus sequences). They report per-family copy numbers and full-length counts across the examined genome assemblies, and approximate the genome portion contributed by full-length SINEs as (copy number × consensus length) / assembly size. Average similarity-to-consensus values were summarized as boxplots (whiskers including all data points). The copy numbers vary markedly across families and genomes; the methods note that, for the partner-LINE search, the assembly with the highest Angio-SINE copy number per order was chosen (Table S3), reflecting substantial between-order variation in proliferation success.

Gene association. Genome annotations (NCBI *_genomic.gff) were used (R packages rtracklayer and GenomicRanges; overlapping annotations collapsed ignoring orientation) to calculate the portion of SINEs, genes and CDS, and to count SINEs located in genes or CDS. A prominent finding is that SolS-II (potato) is enriched in genic regions: its dispersed distribution across all metaphase chromosomes, often enriched in distal gene-rich regions (Wenke et al. 2011), corroborates the bioinformatic observation. The authors suggest regulatory motifs or structural properties of the Angio-domain may have contributed to target-site specificity, tolerance of genic insertions, or even positive selection through beneficial host effects.

Three diversification routes (Figure 7). The superfamily diversified via (1) divergence — most families; (2) dimerization — illustrated by the dimeric SINEs SaliS-V and ProtS-II, in which two Angio-domain-containing units are joined; and (3) modular reshuffling — illustrated by EriS-I and SaliS-IV, where module exchange produced novel combinations. The network analysis (SplitsTree4, GTR distance, Neighbor-Net) on the 24-family alignment (Data S1) visualizes these relationships. The derived/diverged family FabaS-IV (≈83% similar to EriS-III but with a degenerated Angio-domain) is cited as an example of an Angio-SINE-derived family that has lost the diagnostic domain, showing the domain is not invariably retained.

Partner-LINE search and the relaxed-partner hypothesis. Two strategies were used. (i) All 6137 putative non-SINE LINE sequences from Repbase Update (accessed 07.06.2017) were mapped against the 3' SINE region downstream of the eAngio 5' end (Geneious Map-to-Reference, relaxed settings); antisense hits excluded, and candidates required ≥70% similarity in the 3' regions of Angio-domain and LINE. (ii) Eight plant LINE-RT nucleotide HMMs (RTE plus seven L1 subclades: LIb, LINE-CS, PUR, BNR, Karma, cin4, nubo; Heitkam et al. 2014) were run with nhmmer on the highest-copy assembly of each of the 13 orders, yielding 54,998 LINE RT sequences (Table S3) after filtering (length ≥200 bp, bitscore ≥40); 5000 bp up/downstream of each RT was extracted and searched for Angio-domain similarity (BLASTN and BLASTN-short), with three filters (≥70% identity, ≥10 bp length; Angio-domain forward match within 5 kbp downstream of RT; removal of full-length SINE insertions within the LINE), yielding 116 candidate RTs with downstream Angio-domain similarity that were manually evaluated. Neither strategy retrieved a stringent partner LINE sharing the Angio-sequence >70% in its 3' region, so the authors infer a "relaxed" (non-sequence-stringent) partner-LINE relationship.

Evolutionary interpretation. The authors conclude that the structural and sequence conservation of the Angio-domain is the key to the successful proliferation and maintenance of the Angio-SINE superfamily. They offer three non-mutually-exclusive explanations for survival: (1) a relaxed partner-LINE interaction that needs only structural rather than sequence-specific recognition; (2) enhanced retrotranspositional efficiency mediated by the Angio-domain's RNA secondary structure / ribosome interaction (cf. the UnaL2/eel-LINE stem-loop, Baba et al. 2004); and (3) retention driven by frequent integration into genes, possibly beneficial to the host. They stress that the 3'-terminal location, high conservation (exceeding that of the promoter A/B boxes), and predicted stem-loop all point to a functional RNA-level role rather than a mere neutral relic.
