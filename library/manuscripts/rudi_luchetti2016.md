## Summary
RUDI is a newly characterized short interspersed nuclear element (SINE) first isolated from the bivalve molluscs Ruditapes decussatus and R. philippinarum and shown to belong to the V-SINE superfamily — a group previously known only from non-amniote vertebrate SINEs and a single cnidarian SINE, making RUDI the first reported V-domain SINE in invertebrates. Screening of GenBank transcriptome databases and a 454/Illumina molluscan read archive (Smith et al. 2011) revealed the element to be widely but patchily distributed across Mollusca, present in 32 species spanning 14 families and four classes (Bivalvia, Gastropoda, Polyplacophora, Solenogastres), while absent from Monoplacophora, Caudofoveata, Scaphopoda, Cephalopoda and several fully sequenced molluscan genomes. What is genuinely new is threefold: (1) the demonstration that the V highly conserved domain (V HCD) is broadly distributed among invertebrate SINEs, not restricted to vertebrates/cnidarians; (2) evidence from whole-length sequence conservation and comparison with the ancient AmnSINE (~310 Myr) and Au SINE (~320 Myr) families suggesting RUDI is monophyletic and possibly among the most ancient — and the oldest directly characterized — SINE families, predating the ~550 Myr molluscan radiation; and (3) nucleotide-variability and phylogenetic analyses that indicate long-term vertical inheritance as the dominant pattern but single out an apparent horizontal-transfer event between the distantly related bivalves Yoldia limatula and Tegillarca granosa, with vertical inheritance plus possible horizontal-transfer episodes offered as the most parsimonious explanation of the observed distribution.

## Results & Discussion
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION (detailed synthesis)

RUDI discovery and structural characterization. Pairwise comparison of clones from the Ruditapes decussatus repetitive-DNA-enriched library revealed a 265 bp fragment present in two clones (D12 and D25) at 97.3% identity; an additional copy (clone P14F) was found in the R. philippinarum library at 82.7% average identity to the R. decussatus copies. The D25 repeat carries an extra 20 bp poly-(A) segment at one end and is flanked by 12 bp perfect direct repeats (target site duplications, TSDs); no TSDs flanked the D12 and P14F clones. Analysis of the end opposite the A-rich tail revealed a potential secondary structure typical of selenocysteine tRNA with well-conserved RNA polymerase III (polIII) promoters (A and B boxes). Together — a tRNA-related head, anonymous body, AT-rich/poly-(A) tail and TSDs — these features confirmed the element as a SINE, named RUDI. The three Ruditapes clones were deposited under GenBank accession numbers KT809347 (D25), KT809348 (D12) and KT809349 (P14F).

Taxonomic distribution. The RUDI consensus (built from D12, D25, P14F) was used to probe GenBank nr/nt, EST and transcriptome-shotgun-assembly (tsa_nt) databases (as of January 2015) plus the molluscan transcriptome read archive SRA044948 (Smith et al. 2011). In transcriptome databases RUDI was found embedded in larger RNA polII transcripts and recovered in 16 bivalve species (Table 1), with copy number ranging from 4 (Solenaia oleivora, Unionidae) to 85 (Sinonovacula constricta, Solecurtidae); at least two TSD-bearing copies were found in each species. RUDI was also retrieved in the abalone Haliotis (Gastropoda, Haliotidae; 97 copies in Ha. discus) and in the chiton Lepidochitona cinerea (Polyplacophora, Lepidochitonidae; 3 copies). In Ha. discus two structural variants occur — variant 1 (35 copies) and variant 2 (62 copies) — differing by a large deletion in the body region of variant 2 but still sharing 76.5% similarity in shared regions; similar variant-1/variant-2 pairs (differing by the same ~body deletion, 174 bp vs ~277–284 bp) were found in Ha. asinina, Ha. diversicolor and Ha. midae. Probing SRA044948 with species-specific RUDI consensus sequences recovered 9 copies (3 with TSDs) from the slit-shell gastropod Perotrochus lucaya (Pleurotomariidae), 56 matching reads (identity 75–83%, later expanded to 807 reads, identity 73–100%) from the solenogaster Neomenia megatrapezata (Neomeniidae), from which a RUDI contig was assembled despite the short read lengths precluding flanking/TSD recovery. From GenBank genome databases (Table 2) an additional 11 RUDI copies from a further seven bivalves and two Haliotis species were isolated and used only for distribution and phylogenetic analysis (one to two full-length copies each). Aggregated, RUDI is present in 32 mollusc species across 14 families and four classes (Bivalvia, Gastropoda, Polyplacophora, Solenogastres). The distribution is not ubiquitous: no RUDI or RUDI-like sequences were found in Monoplacophora, Caudofoveata, Scaphopoda or Cephalopoda, nor in the sequenced genomes of the bivalves Crassostrea gigas and Pinctada fucata, the gastropods Aplysia californica (AASC00000000.3) and Lottia gigantea (AMQO00000000.1), or the cephalopod Octopus bimaculoides. Searches against Repbase Update (Bao et al. 2015) found no matches to known repeats, confirming RUDI as novel.

Sequence diversity. From the main dataset the authors derived 25 species-specific consensus sequences of 174–284 bp; average within-species divergence (uncorrected p distances) ranged from 8.0% (lowest) to 36.4% (Ha. discus variant 2), with most species 15–30%. Consensus-length variation was driven largely by the variant-2 body deletion (174 bp) versus full-length elements (~226–284 bp), and by indels in the V domain. A histogram of pairwise p distances among bivalve-family consensus sequences was used to inspect the distribution of RUDI diversity.

The V domain and the V-SINE superfamily assignment. Sequence-structure analysis showed RUDI carries the V highly conserved domain (V HCD), which had previously been found only in non-amniote vertebrate SINEs and in a single cnidarian SINE — RUDI is therefore the first V-domain SINE reported from invertebrates and places the element in the V superfamily. The V domain in RUDI is split into two parts by an indel-rich region (indels up to ~30 bp; Supplementary Fig. S1). Comparison of the RUDI V domain to a reference V-domain consensus of vertebrate/cnidarian V-SINEs gave identity values from 48.0% (Pe. lucaya) to 63.9% (L. cinerea) (Supplementary Table S2), with an overall RUDI V-domain identity of ~64% — substantially lower than the vertebrate V domain itself (86–93%), reflecting the great evolutionary depth separating molluscan RUDI from vertebrate V-SINEs. For comparison, the CORE domain of Ceph SINE ranges from ~50% (octopus) to 73% (human) to 98% (platypus) among vertebrates, and the authors note that the V domain's 3' portion is the least conserved.

Monophyly and antiquity. Whole-length conservation across the superfamily, coupled with the shared V domain and conserved tRNA head, suggests a monophyletic origin of RUDI. The overall sequence-conservation pattern resembles that of the ancient AmnSINE (~310 Myr) and Au SINE (~320 Myr) families, which led the authors to argue RUDI could be among the most ancient SINE families and possibly older than ~550 Myr, having originated in a molluscan ancestor in the early Cambrian — making it the oldest SINE characterized to date.

Phylogeny, nucleotide variability and the vertical-inheritance-plus-horizontal-transfer argument. Maximum-likelihood (GTR+G; 100 bootstraps) and Bayesian (MrBayes, 10^7 generations, two runs, PSRF≈1) trees did not resolve high-level molluscan relationships; instead RUDI copies formed mostly species-specific lineages or intermingled clades. The most striking result is the strong, well-supported clustering of Yoldia limatula (Nuculanoida) and Tegillarca granosa (Arcida) — two distantly related bivalves — with high nodal support. Crucially, the nucleotide divergence between these two species falls within the range observed among same-family species, far lower than expected given their deep phylogenetic separation. The authors interpret this incongruence between the TE tree and the host phylogeny, together with the anomalously low divergence, as an indicator of horizontal transfer between Y. limatula and T. granosa. They note that horizontal transfer of SINEs has rarely been documented (e.g., the SmaI SINE family shared by coregonid and salmonid fishes; SINE movement between reptiles and mammals possibly mediated by poxviruses), so direct HT evidence for SINEs is scarce. They consider alternative explanations — such as random lineage-sorting/loss of ancestral copies — but argue that extensive stochastic loss is at odds with the element's broad and persistent distribution. Because RUDI is otherwise largely congruent with host phylogeny, they conclude that long-term vertical inheritance is the primary mode of transmission, with sporadic horizontal-transfer episodes superimposed; this combined model is offered as the most parsimonious explanation for the observed (patchy but widespread) taxonomic distribution.

Evolutionary/functional context of HCDs. The authors frame RUDI within the broader HCD-SINE landscape: the eight metazoan HCDs (LF, CORE, V, Nin, Deu, Inv, Pln, Ceph) plus the plant Au domain, with nested relationships (Inv part of Nin; Nin part of Deu; Deu largely deuterostome-restricted; Nin across all main metazoan phyla except arthropods; Inv in hemichordates, echinoderms and arthropods). The discovery that the V domain is widespread among invertebrate SINEs broadens its known distribution beyond vertebrates and cnidarians. They discuss the two competing hypotheses for HCD persistence — (i) an advantage to the SINE itself via the module-exchange/RNA-template-jumping mechanism (Gilbert and Labuda 2000; Kramerov and Vassetzky 2011), versus (ii) an advantage to the host genome through exaptation as a regulatory domain (Deragon 2012), as exemplified by exapted HCD-SINEs in vertebrates and human Alu. The observation that RUDI copies are embedded in larger RNA polII transcripts is taken as suggestive of a possible regulatory/exaptive role analogous to Alu, and of a capacity for HCD-SINEs to give rise to new copies after long periods of suppressed retroposition. The study thus both expands the known range of the V superfamily and provides a molluscan system for examining the vertical/horizontal dynamics and potential host-level functions of ancient HCD-SINEs.
