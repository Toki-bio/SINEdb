Scan strategy (updated 2026-08-21): keyword search only surfaces papers that announce a novel family name in the title/abstract, which misses two entire classes of relevant work: (1) papers re-evaluating a family SINEBase already has, and (2) papers that describe new families without using words like "novel" up front. The fix is citation snowballing, which doesn't depend on guessing the right keyword:
  • Backward from the database itself: pulled all 176 papers (2015+) that cite the SINEBase paper directly (Vassetzky & Kramerov 2013, NAR) via OpenAlex's cites: filter — virtually every SINE-family paper cites the database it's comparing against, so this one query surfaces most of the field in one shot, independent of what any individual paper calls its new family.
  • Forward, author-by-author: full 2015–2026 output pulled per OpenAlex author ID for each core lab member (Kramerov done; Vassetzky/Ustyantsev/Kosushkin/Borodulina/Luchetti queued), not just their co-authored papers with each other.
  • Every existing-vs-new call is checked against the actual parsed bank catalogue (sinebase_catalog.json, name + taxon fields), not a species-name guess or a plausible-looking match — this caught that SacSINE1/LmeSINE1/CM-1 already exist (so the 2017 chondrichthyan paper is a re-evaluation, not a gap) and that "C" already exists for Lagomorpha (so the 2026 rabbit/pika paper is too).
  • 2nd-order widening (run this pass): pulled citations of 4 newly confirmed new-family papers (Angio-SINE, SINE28, hagfish, Ere) — found 2 more genuine leads (Salicaceae SaliS extension, reptile Sauria re-analysis) purely by following the citation graph one hop further, with no new keywords at all. This step compounds: each round's finds become next round's seeds.
The 1st-order citation-snowball query (176 papers, one request) surfaced 8 of the 12 confirmed new-family rows below, several of which no keyword sweep in this tracker's earlier passes had found (Angio-SINE superfamily, SINE28, 18 amphioxus families, grass SINEs) — direct evidence the earlier keyword-only method was systematically under-counting. The 2nd-order pass added 2 more from just 4 seed papers. 3rd-order + full author-ID sweep completed (2026-08-21): citations of the Salicaceae paper ran dry (methylation/repeat-profiling papers, no new SINE families); Vassetzky/Ustyantsev/Kosushkin/Borodulina's own first-author output overlaps almost entirely with Kramerov's (same core lab, shared co-authorship), no new rows beyond what's already tracked; Luchetti's own output surfaced one borderline case ("Persaeus", a CR1-LINE-derived short internally-deleted element in Lepidoptera) that's arguably outside strict SINE scope (LINE-derived, not tRNA/7SL/rRNA-derived) — noted but not added as a tracker row pending a scope decision. Taxa swept clean (keyword-based, still due for a citation-snowball pass): crustaceans, cnidarians, nematodes, amphibians. Blattella cockroach and HCD-tRNA bivalve papers both resolved (2026-08-21) via direct full-text fetch (both open access, real content pulled and searched, not snippets): Blattella genuinely doesn't name its 9 SINEs individually in the main text (a real negative result); the bivalve paper turned out to be a comparative/distributional survey of the already-known HCD-tRNA superfamily across 40+ genomes, not a new-family-naming paper, so it's reclassified from "unverified" to "existing superfamily, broader survey." New search axis added (2026-08-21): Dfam cross-reference, for "isolated" publications a citation-network search structurally cannot find. Citation-snowballing (from SINEBase or from author networks) only surfaces papers that are already aware of, and cite, the SINE-specific literature - it cannot find a genome paper that ran generic repeat annotation and happened to name a SINE without ever engaging with that literature (the tuatara-genome pattern). Dfam is a much larger (1,510 SINE-type entries vs. SINEBase's 244), broadly-fed database - pulled the full list directly via its API and cross-referenced species/taxon coverage against the bank. Found: a 21-species bivalve/mollusk SINE set and a 2nd-ever fungal SINE entry (Zymoseptoria tritici), both below. Caveat found in the process: most Dfam SINE entries are automated RepeatModeler/SINE_Scan pipeline output from large genome consortiums (DNA Zoo, Zoonomia-style projects) with no individual discovery paper behind them at all - a different problem than "the paper exists but isn't in SINEBase," and arguably belongs more to the separate WGS-silent-annotation task than this citation tracker. Exhaustive Dfam diff completed (2026-08-21): wrote a script comparing all 1,510 Dfam SINE records against all 244 SINEBase entries by taxon (241 species-level gaps found, but ~99% are automated mammalian genome-consortium output with no paper behind them at all). Filtering to non-mammalian taxa surfaced one genuinely important correction: a real nematode SINE (Heligmosomoides, found in a 2023 host-parasite population-genomics preprint) that directly contradicts this tracker's earlier keyword-based "nematodes swept clean" claim - proof that citation/keyword search structurally cannot find papers that never engage with the SINE literature, which is exactly why the Dfam axis was worth adding. Chewed through the remaining non-mammalian Dfam candidates one by one (2026-08-21): of 9 remaining taxa (after excluding the already-resolved bivalve and Lepidoptera batches), checked each Dfam record's own citation field directly - 2 more confirmed gaps (19-species Heliconiine butterfly SINE paper, castor bean), 1 pure-automation exclusion with zero citation at all (Halyomorpha halys stink bug - raw "RepeatModeler Generated" output, no paper exists), and 3 that trace to pre-2015 sources (zebrafish to the 2013 reference genome paper, C. elegans to a 1996 paper, one cichlid entry uncited) - correctly excluded as out of this tracker's 2015+ scope. Correction after user feedback (2026-08-21): "generated" SINEs are not noise to discard, they're candidates awaiting a different verification method. Ran a full per-species citation check (not a sample) across all 241 Dfam gaps, one Dfam API lookup per species. Result: 222 had SOME citation attached, but a deeper look found 185 of those (83%) all cite the exact same paper - Hubley et al. 2016, "Accurate Transposable Element Annotation Is Vital When Analyzing New Genome Assemblies" (PMID 26802115) - which is a methods paper about the RepeatModeler pipeline itself, automatically credited on every pipeline run, not a real per-species discovery paper. That means the true "has a real, specific discovery paper" count is much smaller than 222 - most of the "cited" bucket is exactly as automated as the "uncited" bucket, just with a boilerplate citation attached instead of none. This directly validates the concern: don't treat "has some citation" as proof of real discovery, and don't discard "no citation" as proof of nothing there. Full uncited list (19 species, genuinely zero citation of any kind) is preserved in dfam_uncited_candidates.json and the complete per-species citation breakdown (all 241, cited and uncited) is in dfam_citation_check_report.txt - both committed alongside this tracker, not discarded, for direct genome-level verification/rejection outside this literature-citation workflow. One real find from the citation check: the Zymoseptoria row below now has its actual source paper (was previously untraceable). Crustaceans, cnidarians, and amphibians re-checked via Dfam directly (2026-08-21), not just keyword search. Unlike nematodes (which had zero Dfam SINE entries under a keyword search but a real hit once the exact clade was queried directly), Crustacea and Cnidaria have literally zero Dfam entries of any repeat type resembling a SINE - the handful of entries in those clades are all PLE (Penelope-Like Elements), a completely different retrotransposon class. Amphibia has 69 Dfam entries total but zero are SINE-type (all LTR/ERV) - consistent with the 2024 frog genome paper found earlier, which only reports LINE/L1 content. These three taxa are now confirmed clean via two independent methods (keyword search AND direct database cross-reference), a meaningfully stronger claim than the original keyword-only sweep. Resolving the remaining unverified rows is still open.
